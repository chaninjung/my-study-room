Same matrix as in Week 5? Yes - the asserts confirm the shape, the stratification and the scaling, and in Part D the Week 5 models are refitted on this exact matrix and reproduce their Week 5 scores (DBSCAN silhouette 0.196, Ward 0.177).

Why StandardScaler is not "changing the dataset": the assignment explicitly asks for a pipeline "wherever scaling is needed", and K-means compares Euclidean distances, so without scaling duration_ms (values around 2e5) would completely dominate energy or acousticness (values 0-1). The scaler is fitted inside the pipeline on the same 3,000 tracks and only puts the 15 columns on a comparable scale: no row is added, dropped or reordered, and every model in this notebook (K-means, DBSCAN, Ward) sees the identical X_scaled matrix. Scaling is therefore formatting, not a different dataset.

The three internal evaluation criteria (used for every model below)¶

Criterion Direction What it rewards
Silhouette score higher is better points are close to their own cluster (cohesion) and far from the next one (separation); range [-1, 1]
Calinski-Harabasz index higher is better high between-cluster variance relative to within-cluster variance
Davies-Bouldin index lower is better compact clusters that are far from each other (average worst-case similarity)

Noise rule: DBSCAN may label points as noise (-1). Noise is not a cluster, so it is excluded from all three scores, and the number/percentage of noise points is always reported next to the scores.

Model-selection rule (unchanged from Week 5): a configuration must place at least 5% of the clustered points in its smallest cluster; otherwise a solution that leaves a handful of singletons would win on a technicality (an almost empty cluster is trivially "well separated").

# 10) ONE shared evaluation helper -> the same three criteria for every model
def evaluate(Xs, labels, runtime=np.nan):
    labels = np.asarray(labels)
    noise = int((labels == -1).sum())
    keep = labels != -1
    Xs_eval, lab_eval = Xs[keep], labels[keep]
    n_clusters = len(set(lab_eval))
    sizes = np.sort(np.bincount(lab_eval))[::-1]
    return {
        "n_clusters": n_clusters,
        "n_noise": noise,
        "noise %": round(noise / len(labels) * 100, 2),
        "min cluster %": round(sizes.min() / len(lab_eval) * 100, 2) if len(sizes) else np.nan,
        "cluster sizes": list(sizes),
        "silhouette": round(float(silhouette_score(Xs_eval, lab_eval)), 4),
        "calinski_harabasz": round(float(calinski_harabasz_score(Xs_eval, lab_eval)), 1),
        "davies_bouldin": round(float(davies_bouldin_score(Xs_eval, lab_eval)), 4),
        "runtime (s)": round(float(runtime), 2),
    }

D2a. Did all internal evaluation criteria agree on the best model? No - silhouette and CH pick K-means (0.2075 / 671.4) while Davies-Bouldin picks DBSCAN (1.7226 vs 1.8399), and this does not change when all three models are rescored on the same 2,919 non-noise points (K-means 0.2076 / 702.9 / 1.7850, DBSCAN 0.1956 / 278.2 / 1.7226, Ward 0.1728 / 582.1 / 2.0102).

The criteria reward different geometries: DBSCAN puts 2,651 of its 2,919 points into one loose cluster and 268 into a very tight core, and because Davies-Bouldin averages worst-case cluster ratios, that very tight 268-track core likely explains why DBSCAN wins this criterion, while silhouette and CH reward separation across all points.

The models also disagree structurally - the ARI between K-means k = 2 and DBSCAN is -0.07, DBSCAN's 268-track core coincides with the small K-means k = 3 cluster, and the 81 noise points are spread over all three k = 3 clusters (17 / 20 / 44).

D2b. Strengths and weaknesses of K-means on this dataset. Its strengths are the best silhouette and CH (on the full data and on the shared non-noise points), a partition that repeats for every k-means++ seed, full data coverage, readable centroids, and the simplest tuning (one integer k that the elbow and two of three criteria support).

Its weaknesses are that it has no noise concept (the 81 ambiguous tracks must be forced into a cluster), it assumes roughly convex and similarly sized clusters, and it loses Davies-Bouldin to DBSCAN even on identical points.

Ward is a consistently weaker alternative (worse on all three criteria at k = 2 and k = 3) and needs an O(n^2) linkage, while DBSCAN trades 2.7% of the tracks for a compact core cluster and the best DB score.

Final answer (6 sentences: cluster quality, robustness, interpretability, practicality of tuning, caveat, recommendation)¶

Cluster quality: K-means k = 2 has the best silhouette (0.2075) and CH (671.4) of the three models, and it also wins on the shared 2,919 non-noise points (0.2076 / 702.9 versus DBSCAN 0.1956 / 278.2), although DBSCAN keeps the best Davies-Bouldin there (1.7226 vs 1.7850).

Robustness: its solution is highly stable - the best of 200 random starts and every k-means++ restart give the same 2133 / 867 partition (mean ARI 0.9997), with only a few poor random starts falling into degenerate solutions - whereas DBSCAN changes qualitatively with eps (2651 / 268 at eps = 3.2, a degenerate 2950 + 27 split at eps = 4.0).

Interpretability: the two centroids read directly as a "loud / rhythmic / produced" group versus a "quiet / acoustic / instrumental" group (energy +0.53 vs -1.29, acousticness -0.52 vs +1.29), which matches the genre composition and is easier to act on than DBSCAN's noise flag.

Practicality of tuning: it is the simplest model to tune - one hyperparameter k that the elbow and two of three criteria agree on, fits that run in well under a second (the first call, B1 with 50 restarts, took 1.7 s), and all 3,000 tracks assigned.

Caveat: the structure is weak (silhouette ~0.21, two heavily overlapping blobs), so the clusters are a stylistic gradient rather than clean genres, and no value of k or extra initialisation raises these metrics.

Recommendation: use K-means k = 2 as the primary model, K-means k = 3 when the explicit, danceable core (285 tracks, 116 of them hip-hop) has to be separated, and DBSCAN (eps = 3.2, min_samples = 10) only as a complementary outlier detector.

All models above were fitted on the identical 3,000 x 15 X_scaled matrix produced by the Week 5 cleaning pipeline; only the clustering algorithms and their parameters differ. Metrics were computed with scikit-learn's silhouette_score, calinski_harabasz_score and davies_bouldin_score; DBSCAN noise points were excluded from scoring and reported separately.
